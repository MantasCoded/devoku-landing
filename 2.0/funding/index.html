Devoku raises $9M in private funding

Devoku, a startup that sells what it calls digital employees, has raised $9 million from its main investor and engineer: Daumantas Vinkevicius.

"AI should not replace humans in a literal existential sense," he said. "Instead, one person should run an army of AI employees, extending your own work and ideas 100 to 1,000x and letting you concentrate on what matters to you — whether that's your personal freedom, scaling, or new matter and possibilities that don't even exist yet but that you can bring into existence. For the first time in history, what matters most is your creativity, intellect, beliefs, persistence and judgment, which you can multiply many times over by having digital employees reflect them and automate everything for you, 24/7."

Devoku's digital employees are not assistants that advise you or search for information on how to do the job. Each employee runs a real workspace on a persistent Devoku Computer and does the job for you. They work with their own files, a real browser they can log into and operate, and a memory that keeps the rules, knowledge and credentials a given piece of work depends on. A user describes the task once, in ordinary language. The employee works out a method, retains it, and repeats the work later without being rebuilt from scratch. And everything is controllable through a simple conversation with a digital employee — by writing or by sending voice messages — as if you were naturally chatting with a real human.

"An automation should be a conversation, not a flowchart or DIY connectors," Vinkevicius said. "A flowchart is static, and every extra idea needs additional incorporation. A conversation means that every new idea you have needs only one text or voice message, and the automation adjusts itself intellectually. Flowcharts were built for the pre-AI era, and people who never knew how to code are suddenly able to create those static flowcharts with AI vibecoding — but that's still an ancient concept in today's world, and it doesn't let you actually utilize the intelligence that AI is designed to bring. Devoku fixes that gap: you speak with an agent about what you want, it designs and builds the infrastructure for itself, and you achieve the goals instead of designing the structures to achieve them yourself. Let the AI self-construct."

Under the hood, Devoku runs its own Devoku Jet as well as Claude Code, Cursor and Codex, in a single normalized tool-call and chat layer owned and built by Devoku. That layer solves the biggest issue developers face today when using these tools individually: accessibility, persistence, security and replication.

"Claude Code, Cursor and Codex are great — our whole team used to run multiple sessions on multiple screens at once," Vinkevicius said. "But they don't solve four essential pillars: accessibility, persistence, security and replication. They are apps designed to run on your personal computer while your computer is alive, or slim cloud agents that do not guarantee persistence, tool versatility and replication. What if you want to access your agents from any device, anytime, while you are not at your computer? What if you want your agents to work 24/7 without any loss of data or progress? What if you want agents to talk to each other? What if you want to give your passwords to AI — you just paste them into an agent's chat and expect them to be secure? What if you want to switch from Claude Code to Cursor and not lose a single bit of working memory? What if you want to multiply your agents by 1,000x instantly? What if you want to chat with humans, as well as agents in the same place? All of this is what Devoku is built to solve. It takes what's great about using agent software locally and builds all the complex cloud, orchestration, accessibility, persistence, replication and security layers on top that you'd otherwise need to build yourself directly with Claude, Cursor or Codex," Vinkevicius said. "Effectively, we built a layer that lets you scale instantly and gain your personal freedom at the same time. You should not be glued to your computer. You should run AI agents on Devoku computers by simply chatting with them. I gained my freedom. And we are happy to share it with the world. And we will let Devoku improve and self-construct itself every day."
